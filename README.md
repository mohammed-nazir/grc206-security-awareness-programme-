> **Academic-use notice:** This repository contains coursework based on a fictional organisation. It is not an operational security programme and contains no real employee records, credentials, incident evidence, or regulated customer data.

Project Overview
This repository contains the GRC206 assignment Design a Security Awareness Programme, developed around the fictional organisation Northbridge Logistics.

The project proposes a practical, risk-based security awareness programme for 800 employees working across head office, warehouses and remote sales operations. It responds to the scenario's invoice-fraud attempts, password reuse following a breach, and responsibility for regulated customer information.

The programme follows a continuous five-stage lifecycle:

Assess → Plan → Deliver → Measure → Improve

Its central purpose is to improve security-related employee behaviour, support early reporting and reduce human-related security risks over a 12-month period. The approach is designed to remain workable for an organisation with a small security team and a modest training budget.

Organisational Scenario
Northbridge Logistics has 800 employees across three working environments:
- Head office
- Warehouses
- Remote sales operations
The scenario identifies recent invoice-fraud attempts and two employees who reused passwords after a breach. The organisation also handles regulated customer data, while its security team and training budget are limited.

The project distinguishes confirmed scenario facts from planning assumptions. In particular, the employee numbers assigned to audience groups are illustrative estimates and must be validated by management before implementation.
Programme Aim
To reduce human-related security risks by helping employees recognise threats, make safer decisions and report suspicious activity promptly through short, practical and role-specific learning activities.
Priority Human-Related Risks
Risk	Scenario basis	Desired safer behaviour
Invoice fraud and business email compromise	Recent invoice-fraud attempts	Independently verify payment instructions and supplier-detail changes; report suspicious messages.
Password reuse and credential compromise	Two employees reused passwords after a breach	Use unique passwords, MFA and safe authentication practices.
Accidental exposure or mishandling of regulated customer information	The organisation handles regulated customer data	Handle, store and share customer information securely.


The third risk is a reasoned priority based on the organisation's data-handling responsibilities; the scenario does not state that a customer-data breach has already occurred.
Employee Audiences
The programme uses role-based learning rather than relying on one generic session for every employee.
Audience	Illustrative population	Main learning emphasis
Finance, Accounts Payable and Procurement	60	Invoice verification, supplier impersonation, payment-change controls and reporting.
Warehouse and Logistics Employees	350	Suspicious messages, device security, operational records and accessible reporting.
Remote Sales and Customer-Facing Employees	180	Password security, MFA, secure remote access and customer-data protection.
Head Office and General Administrative Employees	170	Email security, data classification, secure document handling and incident reporting.
Executives, Managers and IT/Security Personnel	40	Executive impersonation, secure approvals, privileged access and incident escalation.
Total	800	


Population figures are planning assumptions, not staffing data provided by the case scenario.
Measurable Programme Objectives
The assignment proposes three measurable objectives. Targets should be confirmed or adjusted after the Month 1 baseline assessment.
1. Phishing resistance: Reduce the phishing simulation click rate by at least 50% relative to the Month 1 baseline and increase the reporting rate to at least 70% by Month 12.
2. Password and account security: Achieve at least 95% MFA coverage for in-scope accounts where supported, and at least 85% correct responses in a practical password and MFA assessment by Month 9.
3. Customer-data handling: Achieve at least 90% correct responses in a practical data-handling assessment and reduce recorded human-related data-handling incidents by at least 25% against baseline by Month 12, where reliable incident records are available.
Delivery Approach
The programme combines methods suited to different roles and work settings:
- Quarterly short digital awareness modules.
- Monthly warehouse toolbox talks and shift briefings.
- Quarterly finance fraud-prevention workshops.
- Baseline and quarterly phishing simulations.
- Posters and awareness notices refreshed quarterly.
- Quarterly management and executive briefings.
- Twice-yearly practical data-handling exercises.
- Security awareness induction for new employees during their first working week.
Phishing simulations are intended as learning and measurement exercises. Employees who interact with a simulation should receive supportive feedback and guidance, not public embarrassment. The proposed no-blame reporting routes include a dedicated security email, the IT helpdesk or ticketing system, supervisors and telephone contact where digital channels are unavailable.
Measurement Framework
The project measures programme reach, employee behaviour and security outcomes.
Metric	What it indicates
Training completion rate	Programme reach; completion alone does not demonstrate competence.
Phishing click rate	Employee response to controlled phishing simulations.
Phishing reporting rate	Employees' ability and willingness to report simulated suspicious messages.
MFA coverage	Adoption of an additional authentication control for in-scope accounts.
Secure data-handling assessment score	Ability to make safe decisions in practical scenarios.
Human-related security incident trend	Recorded incident patterns compared with baseline and equivalent periods.


Results should be interpreted together. For example, an increase in incident reports may reflect greater employee confidence in reporting rather than an increase in actual security failures. Simulation results are also controlled indicators, not a complete representation of real-world attack behaviour.
Governance and Continuous Improvement
The proposed programme owner is the Security/GRC Lead, with the COO or equivalent acting as executive sponsor. IT, HR, Finance, departmental and warehouse supervisors, and a data protection or compliance representative support delivery and measurement.

Formal programme reviews take place at the end of Months 3, 6, 9 and 12. Reviews consider training completion, simulation results, MFA coverage, assessment scores, incident trends, employee feedback, progress against objectives and available resources.

Each review should produce an action log recording the issue, agreed action, owner, due date and status. Training should be refreshed when incidents, emerging threats, repeated poor performance, policy changes, employee feedback or changes in work arrangements show that existing material needs updating.

Twelve-Month Programme at a Glance
Month	Main activity
1	Establish baseline, review incident records, run initial phishing simulation and confirm reporting channels.
2	Launch core awareness training on phishing, passwords, MFA and customer-data protection.
3	Run invoice-fraud workshop, follow-up phishing simulation and first quarterly review.
4	Deliver warehouse toolbox talks on suspicious messages, device security and records.
5	Train remote sales teams on secure remote working and customer-data protection.
6	Run second phishing simulation, review MFA coverage and conduct mid-year review.
7	Practise supplier impersonation and payment-change verification.
8	Deliver data-handling and secure file-sharing refresher exercises.
9	Run third phishing simulation and quarterly programme review.
10	Deliver targeted refresher training based on evidence and identified weaknesses.
11	Collect employee feedback and review onboarding and reporting arrangements.
12	Conduct final simulation, annual assessment and fourth quarterly review.


Monthly training-completion and incident monitoring, new-employee induction, accessible reporting channels and supervisor reinforcement continue throughout the year.

Critical Reflection
The design challenge was creating a programme that addresses human risks across 800 employees without overloading Northbridge Logistics' security team or training budget. A single annual presentation would be inexpensive but would not meet the needs of finance staff, warehouse workers, remote sales employees and managers. I therefore chose role-based activities, using existing supervisors, communication channels and departmental meetings to extend delivery.

Another challenge was measuring behaviour change rather than treating attendance as success. The programme combines phishing click and reporting rates, MFA coverage, data-handling assessments and incident trends, compared with a pre-launch baseline. These measures have limitations: for example, increased reporting may indicate greater awareness rather than more incidents.

I also treated staffing distributions and numerical targets as proposals because the brief does not provide departmental figures or baseline results. Quarterly reviews allow the organisation to adjust activities using evidence while keeping the programme affordable and sustainable.
